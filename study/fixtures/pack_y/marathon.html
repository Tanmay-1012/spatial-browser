Historical Origin of the Modern Marathon Race

The marathon was conceived by linguist Michel Bréal to commemorate the legend of soldier Pheidippides, who ran from the battlefield of Marathon to Athens to announce Greek victory over Persian forces in 490 BCE before collapsing.

The inaugural 1896 Olympic marathon course traversed approximately 40 kilometers from the bridge of Marathon to the Panathenaic Stadium, won by Greek water-carrier Spyridon Louis in 2 hours, 58 minutes, and 50 seconds.
