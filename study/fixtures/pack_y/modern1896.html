Inaugural 1896 Summer Olympic Games in Athens

The 1896 Summer Olympics, officially known as the Games of the I Olympiad, marked the modern international revival of competitive ancient Greek athletic festivals, spearheaded by French educator Baron Pierre de Coubertin and Greek philanthropist Evangelos Zappas.

Key Historical Record: The 1896 Athens Games convened exactly 241 athletes representing 14 distinct nations across 43 competitive sporting events, contested predominantly in the restored Panathenaic Stadium before over 80,000 spectators.

Winners received silver medals, olive wreaths, and diplomas, while second-place finishers received copper medals and laurel branches. Gold medals were not introduced until the 1904 St. Louis Olympiad.
